*Reactive post, drafted 2026-08-02. Broadened from a single NSF DCL to the whole

discovery — of the world, and of yourself — and they are interdependent.*

## Main post (paste-ready, ~2,680 chars)

Federal AI-for-science funding just moved, and it moved big.

The Genesis Mission has committed over $5 billion. First awards landed 22 July: $293M across 278 projects at 342 institutions. NSF issued a Dear Colleague Letter the same week inviting proposals under it. NIH launched Bio Genesis Mission, with the stated goal of doubling the pace of biomedical innovation.

This is the right thing to fund. I want it to work.

📌 But there are two kinds of discovery, and we are only paying for one.

The first is discovery about how the world works — the mechanism, the molecule, the model. That is what this money buys, and AI will genuinely accelerate it.

The second is discovery about yourself. What actually moves your sleep. Whether the thing you started in March did anything. What you would have to notice to stop.

Neither works without the other. A finding about the world does nothing until a specific person discovers something about themselves that makes them act on it. And self-knowledge without evidence is just confident guessing. We are funding one of them.

🔹 Genesis Mission: **$5B+** committed.
🔹 NAIRR, the shared infrastructure for actually using AI in research: **$30M** enacted for FY2026. Roughly 0.6% of that.
🔹 AHRQ — the agency chartered to study whether research changes practice — has **$345M appropriated and has spent under $15M**. No new grants in over a year. 104 grants halted in July. Staff down from about 300 to under 90.

Real exceptions exist and deserve naming, because the absolute version of this claim is wrong. CMS's ACCESS Model began its first cohort on 1 July and **withholds half of provider payment against measured patient outcomes**. PCORI's implementation arm runs $50M across 34 states. Both are real. Both are about 1% of their portfolios — and PCORI's per-project cap fell 28% this cycle.

👉 The tell is at NIST. Its AI standards center lists *"what are reproducible experimental methods to measure AI-driven changes in downstream outcomes over time"* as an **open research question** — on a blog, under a heading of things it is soliciting ideas about. In the same year it stood up a program to benchmark model accuracy.

We know how to measure whether the model got the answer right. We do not yet know how to measure whether anyone's life changed.

Both are discovery. One has a budget line.

💡 Information can be health — but only once it reaches a person who can use it, and only if we ever find out whether it did.

**The counterexamples — worth reading before you agree with me**

• PCORI Health Systems Implementation Initiative: $50M committed, ~$37M awarded across 24–25 projects, now 34 states and DC. PCORI's own framing is the estimated 17-year lag between publication and uptake. https://www.pcori.org/funding-opportunities

• NIST CAISI research blog, "A selection of open AI measurement science questions": https://www.nist.gov/caisi

**AHRQ**: appropriated $345M for FY2026; Congress rejected the proposed merger. The operational figures above are from AHRQ's own docket and server. The agency exists — that matters, and the sloppier version of this point ("AHRQ was abolished") is false.

## Notes for posting

- **Do not post an absolute claim.** "Nothing federal funds downstream outcome
  measurement" is falsifiable in one search — CMS ACCESS withholds half of
  provider payment against measured outcomes and started five weeks ago. The
  post names it before a critic does. The defensible claim is proportional:
  ~1% of the relevant portfolios, and shrinking.
- **AHRQ exists.** Congress rejected the merger and appropriated $345M. The
  point is the gap between appropriation and operation, not abolition.
- **The two-kinds-of-discovery frame is the spine**, not the funding numbers.
  The numbers are evidence for it. Lead with the idea, and let the arithmetic
  land underneath.
- **Generous first.** NSF and DOE are funding the right thing. The turn does not
  arrive until after that is said plainly and meant.
- Unverified and deliberately excluded: Bridge2AI Stage 2 dollar figures, the
  NAIRR-OC award, ARPA-H per-program budgets, any claim about FDA's Elsa beyond
  its existence, and any WISeR evaluation detail.
